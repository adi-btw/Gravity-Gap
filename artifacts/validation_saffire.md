# V3 SAFFIRE-II hindcast (microgravity -> 1 g)

Generated by `python -m engine.hindcast` (seed 0, 2000 Monte Carlo draws). Data: `data/clean/psi_saffire_ug_vs_1g.csv (PSI-99 SAFFIRE-II)`. Machine-readable results: `artifacts/hindcast_saffire.json`.

## Plain-language summary

SAFFIRE-II burned 9 material samples in orbit (on Cygnus) and the same materials on the ground, all at about 22% O2 and a 20 cm/s airflow. We asked: knowing only the orbit result, can the physics bridge tell whether the ground (1 g) result would be the same or different?

- **Bridge (primary, L = sample thickness): 7/9 correct (78%).** Naive baseline "1 g = orbit outcome": 3/9 (33%).
- **Caveat that matters:** with L = thickness every sample lands in the same 1 g regime (mixed), so the bridge gives the same verdict ("buoyancy matters, expect more burning at 1 g") for all 9. Its score equals a constant guess "1 g burns more, unless the orbit sample already burned fully" (7/9). The bridge gets the direction right for these samples, but this test cannot show that it tells samples apart.
- The score depends on how the **mixed** regime is read. If mixed counts as "transfers" (strict variant), the bridge falls to the baseline: 3/9. With L = sample width (50 mm) every sample is buoyant-dominated and both variants give 7/9.
- The bridge's main point holds: at 1 g with 20 cm/s flow, buoyancy is **not** negligible (Ri is around 0.1 to 10 for thin sheets and above 10 at the 50 mm sample width), so orbit results should not be copied to Earth. The data agree: only 3 of 9 samples behaved the same.
- Misses: 2-3 (1.03 mm silicone, did not burn either way) and 2-7 (PMMA+Nomex, burned more in orbit). The bridge cannot predict extinction limits or mixed-material effects.
- At lunar gravity (1.62 m/s2) the thinnest samples move toward forced-flow, so the bridge would let the orbit result transfer for them on the Moon. That is a prediction with no test to check it against.

## Bridge rule

- dominant regime at 1 g: forced if forced fraction >= 0.5, buoyant if buoyant >= 0.5, else mixed
- forced -> ug outcome transfers (predict same)
- buoyant -> ug outcome does not transfer (predict more burn at 1 g)
- mixed -> conservative: more burn at 1 g; strict: same
- ceiling: if ug burned completely, 'more at 1 g' becomes 'same'
- Characteristic length: **sample thickness** is the primary L (it sets the thermal boundary layer of a thin sheet). Sensitivity: L = 10 mm and L = sample width (50 mm). Sample 2-8 has no thickness in the source ("See Fig. 5"); it uses a flagged 10 mm fallback (as for the 1 cm PMMA sample 2-9).
- Conditions: g = 9.81 (Earth) and 1.62 (Moon) m/s2, ~1 atm (not in the table), 20 cm/s forced flow, sample O2.
- Classes: `same`, `more_at_1g`, `more_at_ug` (from `which_burned_more`). "Transfer yes/no" scoring only asks whether the outcome changed, not the direction.

## Limitations

- **n = 9** (6 unflagged). One hit more or less moves accuracy by 11 points. No result here is statistically significant.
- **No discrimination.** The 1 g regime is the same for every sample at a given L, because all samples share the same flow and similar thickness. The test checks the direction of the gravity effect, not the bridge's ability to rank materials.
- The ceiling rule (a fully burned orbit sample cannot burn more) carries part of the score (2-5, 2-6). It uses only orbit data, but it is logic, not physics.
- The bridge has no extinction or flammability-limit physics: it cannot say that 1.03 mm silicone (2-3) stays non-flammable at 1 g while 0.61 mm (2-2) partly burns.
- Mixed-material sample 2-7 (PMMA burned out, Nomex never ignited) is outside the model.
- Pressure is not in the SAFFIRE-II table; ~1 atm is assumed. O2 is derived from CO2 production (source note 1).
- Ground tests may differ from orbit tests in igniter, chamber and sample holder, not only gravity.
- The Moon column is a prediction only. No partial-gravity SAFFIRE data exist.
